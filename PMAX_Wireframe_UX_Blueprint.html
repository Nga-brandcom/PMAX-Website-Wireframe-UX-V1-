02 · Primary user flow

CMO RESEARCH JOURNEY

A typical enterprise buyer doesn't land on a service page first — they land via a search, a referral, or thought-leadership content. The journey moves from belief → proof → fit → conversation. Every page on the site exists to advance one of these steps.

01 · TRIGGER

Discovery

SEO query · referral · LinkedIn post · event · paid campaign · word of mouth.

02 · BELIEF

Read the POV

Homepage thesis, manifesto page, or featured insight establishes PMAX has something to say.

03 · PROOF

See the work

One featured case study with business outcome convinces them this isn't theory.

04 · FIT

Find relevance

Industry page or solution page shows PMAX understands their sector and need.

05 · ACT

Engage

Brief us · book a session · subscribe to insights · join the team. Segmented CTA.

Three intent paths the site must serve

Research-mode — read, download, subscribe. CTA = thinking subscription.
Evaluate-mode — compare, validate, qualify. CTA = book strategy session.
Brief-mode — RFP, partnership, urgent need. CTA = brief us / contact lead.

Homepage — key moves

  1. POV-led heroOne ownable sentence. No service list, no stat-block clutter.
  2. Two-CTA modelPrimary: read thesis. Secondary: see work. No budget form on first contact.
  3. Work in row 2Four hero case studies above the fold. The work is the credential.
  4. Industry as navSector pills as a buyer-led entry. CMOs find their world in one scroll.
  5. Thinking as proofThree pieces of original IP — eBooks, frameworks, reports. Not blog headlines.
  6. Named leadershipReal faces, real voices. Trust signal for enterprise buyers.
  7. Segmented closing CTAThree intent paths in the final band — brief, book, subscribe.

Case study — design rules

  1. Outcome firstHeadline carries the result. Don't bury the metric.
  2. Metric strip up frontThree numbers, ranked by business meaning — not three vanity awards.
  3. Client perspectiveTell the story from the client's problem, not from PMAX's process.
  4. One quote, real faceOne named, photographed client. No anonymous logos.
  5. Industry lead CTAClose with a specific named contact for that industry, not a generic form.
  6. Re-rankableFilter taxonomy: industry · capability · outcome type · year · award.
